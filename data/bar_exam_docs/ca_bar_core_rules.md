## CONTRACTS

### Formation
A valid contract requires: (1) mutual assent (offer + acceptance), (2) consideration, (3) capacity, and (4) legality.

**Offer**: A manifestation of willingness to enter into a bargain that would justify another person in understanding that assent is invited and will conclude the deal. (Restatement §24)
- Must be definite and certain in its terms
- Distinguished from preliminary negotiations, advertisements (generally), and invitations to bid

**Acceptance**: A manifestation of assent to the terms of an offer made in the manner invited by the offer. (Restatement §50)
- Mirror image rule (common law): acceptance must match offer exactly; any variation = counteroffer
- UCC §2-207 (Battle of the Forms): acceptance with additional terms is still acceptance (between merchants, additional terms become part of contract unless material)

**Consideration**: A bargained-for exchange where each party incurs a legal detriment or receives a legal benefit.
- Past consideration is not consideration
- Pre-existing duty rule: performing an existing obligation is not new consideration (but see UCC §2-209: no consideration needed for modification)
- Promissory estoppel (Restatement §90): substitute for consideration when promisor should reasonably expect reliance

### Statute of Frauds (California Civil Code §1624)
Must be in writing: (MYLEGS)
- Marriage promises
- Year: contracts that cannot be performed within 1 year
- Land: interests in real property
- Executor: promises to pay estate debts
- Goods: sale of goods ≥ $500 (UCC §2-201)
- Surety: promises to answer for another's debt

### Parol Evidence Rule
When parties have a fully integrated written agreement, extrinsic evidence of prior or contemporaneous oral agreements cannot contradict the writing.
- Partial integration: consistent additional terms can supplement
- California follows the "reasonably susceptible" test (Pacific Gas & Electric v. Thomas Drayage)
- Exceptions: fraud, duress, mistake, condition precedent, ambiguity

### Breach & Remedies
- Material breach: non-breaching party may suspend performance and sue for damages
- Minor breach: non-breaching party must continue performance but can sue for damages
- Anticipatory repudiation: clear statement of intent not to perform (UCC §2-610)

**Damages:**
- Expectation damages: put plaintiff in position if contract performed (Restatement §347)
- Consequential damages: foreseeable at time of contracting (Hadley v. Baxendale)
- Reliance damages: put plaintiff in position before contract
- Specific performance: when legal remedy inadequate (unique goods, real property)

## TORTS

### Negligence
Elements: (1) Duty, (2) Breach, (3) Causation (actual + proximate), (4) Damages

**Duty**: Generally, duty of reasonable care to foreseeable plaintiffs (Palsgraf analysis)
- California: Rowland v. Christian factors for duty determination
- Professionals: held to standard of profession (doctors, lawyers)
- Landowners (CA): general duty of care to all entrants (abolished trespasser/licensee/invitee distinction)

**Breach**: Failure to act as a reasonably prudent person under the circumstances
- Learned Hand formula: B < P × L (burden < probability × gravity of loss)
- Negligence per se: violation of statute designed to protect plaintiff's class from the type of harm

**Causation**:
- Actual cause: "but for" test; substantial factor test (when multiple causes)
- Proximate cause: foreseeability of harm (no superseding intervening cause)

**Damages**: Must prove actual damages (physical injury, property damage, economic loss)

### Strict Liability
- Abnormally dangerous activities (Restatement §520 factors)
- Products liability (Restatement 3d): manufacturing defect, design defect (consumer expectation OR risk-utility), failure to warn
- Wild animals

### Intentional Torts
- Battery: intentional harmful or offensive contact
- Assault: intentional apprehension of imminent harmful contact
- False imprisonment: intentional confinement without consent
- IIED: extreme and outrageous conduct intentionally/recklessly causing severe emotional distress
- Trespass to land, trespass to chattels, conversion

## CONSTITUTIONAL LAW

### Equal Protection (14th Amendment)
Three tiers of scrutiny:
1. **Strict scrutiny** (race, national origin, alienage): necessary to achieve compelling government interest
2. **Intermediate scrutiny** (gender, legitimacy): substantially related to important government interest
3. **Rational basis** (all other classifications): rationally related to legitimate government interest

### Due Process
- **Substantive due process**: fundamental rights (privacy, marriage, family, contraception, travel)
   - Strict scrutiny for fundamental rights
   - Rational basis for economic/social legislation
- **Procedural due process**: Mathews v. Eldridge balancing test (private interest, risk of error, government interest)

### First Amendment
- Freedom of speech: content-based restrictions get strict scrutiny; content-neutral get intermediate
- Time, place, manner restrictions in public forums: content-neutral, narrowly tailored, leave open alternatives
- Categories of unprotected speech: obscenity (Miller test), fighting words, true threats, incitement (Brandenburg)
- Commercial speech: Central Hudson test (4-part)

### Commerce Clause
- Congress can regulate: channels, instrumentalities, and activities with substantial effect on interstate commerce
- Dormant Commerce Clause: states cannot discriminate against or unduly burden interstate commerce

## CRIMINAL LAW

### Homicide
**Murder**: unlawful killing with malice aforethought
- 1st degree: premeditated/deliberate, felony murder (enumerated felonies under CA Penal Code §189)
- 2nd degree: intentional but not premeditated, depraved heart, non-enumerated felony murder
- Malice: intent to kill, intent to cause serious bodily harm, depraved heart, felony murder

**Voluntary Manslaughter**: intentional killing with adequate provocation (heat of passion)
- Requires: provocation that would arouse sudden passion in reasonable person, no cooling off

**Involuntary Manslaughter**: unintentional killing from criminal negligence or unlawful act

**California Specific**: 
- Felony murder rule (PC §189): during enumerated felonies (arson, rape, carjacking, robbery, burglary, mayhem, kidnapping, train wrecking)
- SB 1437: principals must have been actual killer, aided with intent to kill, or was major participant acting with reckless indifference

### Theft Crimes
- **Larceny**: trespassory taking and carrying away of personal property with intent to permanently deprive
- **Robbery**: larceny from person by force or threat (California: PC §211)
- **Burglary**: entry into structure with intent to commit felony (California: PC §459 – no breaking required)
- **Embezzlement**: fraudulent conversion of property by one in lawful possession

### Inchoate Crimes
- **Attempt**: specific intent + substantial step (MPC) / dangerous proximity (common law)
- **Conspiracy**: agreement between 2+ persons + intent + overt act (California requires overt act)
- **Solicitation**: asking/encouraging another to commit a crime

## EVIDENCE (California Evidence Code)

### Relevance
- CEC §210: relevant evidence = tendency to prove/disprove disputed fact
- CEC §352: court may exclude if probative value substantially outweighed by prejudice (cf. FRE 403)

### Hearsay (CEC §1200)
Out-of-court statement offered to prove truth of the matter asserted.
**Key exceptions:**
- Prior inconsistent statement (CEC §1235 – broader than FRE: admissible for truth in CA)
- Admission of party-opponent (CEC §1220)
- Dying declaration (CEC §1242)
- Business records (CEC §1271)
- State of mind (CEC §1250)
- Spontaneous statement (CEC §1240)
- Past recollection recorded (CEC §1237)

### Character Evidence
- CEC §1101(a): character evidence generally inadmissible to prove conduct
- CEC §1101(b): prior acts admissible for MIMIC (motive, intent, mistake, identity, common plan)
- CEC §1103: defendant may introduce victim's character in criminal case

### Privileges (CA specific)
- Attorney-client (CEC §954)
- Spousal testimonial privilege (CEC §970) + marital communications (CEC §980)
- Physician-patient (CEC §994) – broader in CA than federal
- Psychotherapist-patient (CEC §1014)

## COMMUNITY PROPERTY (California)

### Classification
- **Community property**: all property acquired during marriage from earnings/effort
- **Separate property**: property owned before marriage, acquired by gift/inheritance during marriage, or after separation
- Presumption: all property acquired during marriage is community property (Family Code §760)

### Division
- Equal division upon dissolution (Family Code §2550)
- Exceptions: education loans, personal injury awards, misappropriation by one spouse

### Transmutation (Family Code §852)
- Requires writing + express declaration of change in ownership character
- Exception: gifts of personal property of non-substantial value

## REAL PROPERTY

### Estates in Land
- Fee simple absolute: "O to A and his heirs" (full ownership)
- Fee simple defeasible: determinable (automatically reverts), subject to condition subsequent (right of re-entry), subject to executory limitation
- Life estate: "O to A for life, then to B"
- Future interests: reversion, remainder (vested/contingent), executory interest

### Recording Acts
- Race: first to record wins
- Notice: subsequent BFP without notice wins
- Race-notice: subsequent BFP who records first wins (CALIFORNIA uses race-notice)

### Landlord-Tenant
- Types: tenancy for years, periodic, at will, at sufferance
- Implied warranty of habitability (Green v. Superior Court – California landmark)
- Retaliatory eviction prohibited
